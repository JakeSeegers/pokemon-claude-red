# Study case tables to review

These 27 tables in `src/data/dxcases_more.js` add the DSM-5-TR diagnoses that the study summary does not have. They are written in our own words as a study aid and are marked `draft: true`. In the game, the case notes for these cases say "Draft table: check it against the book."

To approve a table, check it against the DSM-5-TR and remove its `draft: true` line. To fix one, edit the file. This list is built from that file, so rebuild it after a change.

## Neurodevelopmental Disorders

### Stereotypic Movement Disorder

**Core:** Repetitive, seemingly driven, purposeless movements (for example, hand flapping, body rocking, head banging) that interfere with activities or cause self-injury. Onset in the early developmental period.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Normal development:** simple stereotypies in a young child that do not impair function and fade with age.
2. **Autism Spectrum Disorder:** social communication deficits are also present (then specify the stereotypies within autism unless self-injury is the focus).
3. **Tic Disorders:** movements are sudden, nonrhythmic, change over time, and follow a premonitory urge; stereotypies are rhythmic, fixed and start younger.
4. **OCD:** movements are done to neutralize an obsession or by rigid rules.
5. **Trichotillomania or Excoriation Disorder:** the behavior is limited to hair pulling or skin picking.
6. **Substance-induced repetitive behavior (for example, stimulants):** the movements start with the substance and stop after it stops.

## Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders

### Psychotic Disorder Induced by a Substance or Medical Condition

**Members:** Substance/Medication-Induced Psychotic Disorder; Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

**Core:** Prominent hallucinations or delusions, with evidence from history, exam or tests that a substance (intoxication, withdrawal or medication) or a medical condition directly causes them. Not only during delirium.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Substance intoxication or withdrawal alone:** the person knows the perceptions come from the substance (insight kept), so no psychosis is diagnosed.
2. **Primary psychotic disorder (for example, Schizophrenia):** psychosis started before the substance or illness, or lasts well beyond about 1 month after it stops.
3. **Delirium:** attention and awareness are disturbed and fluctuate.
4. **Mood disorder with psychotic features:** psychosis occurs only during a major mood episode.
5. **The other cause (medical vs. substance):** the timeline links the symptoms to the other cause instead.

## Bipolar and Related Disorders

### Bipolar Disorder Induced by a Substance or Medical Condition

**Members:** Substance/Medication-Induced Bipolar and Related Disorder; Bipolar and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

**Core:** Elevated, expansive or irritable mood with increased energy, caused directly by a substance or medication (for example, steroids, stimulants) or a medical condition (for example, hyperthyroidism, Cushing's).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Bipolar I or II Disorder:** mood episodes also occur without the substance or medical cause, or a full mania continues well after the physiological effect of an antidepressant ends.
2. **Delirium:** disturbed attention and awareness that fluctuate.
3. **Substance intoxication alone:** the mood change is expected for the intoxication and not more severe.
4. **Personality Change Due to Another Medical Condition:** a lasting change in personality traits, not distinct mood episodes.

## Depressive Disorders

### Depressive Disorder Induced by a Substance or Medical Condition

**Members:** Substance/Medication-Induced Depressive Disorder; Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

**Core:** Prominent depressed mood or loss of interest, caused directly by a substance or medication (for example, alcohol, interferon, beta blockers) or a medical condition (for example, hypothyroidism, stroke, Parkinson's disease).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Major Depressive Disorder:** the depression began before the substance or illness, or continues for a long time (about 1 month or more) after it stops or is treated.
2. **Adjustment Disorder with depressed mood:** the mood is a psychological reaction to having the illness, not a direct physiological effect.
3. **Delirium:** disturbed attention and awareness that fluctuate.
4. **Substance intoxication or withdrawal alone:** the low mood is expected for the intoxication or withdrawal and is not more severe.

## Anxiety Disorders

### Anxiety Disorder Induced by a Substance or Medical Condition

**Members:** Substance/Medication-Induced Anxiety Disorder; Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

**Core:** Prominent panic attacks or anxiety, caused directly by a substance or medication (for example, caffeine, stimulants, withdrawal from sedatives) or a medical condition (for example, hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, arrhythmia).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Primary anxiety disorder (for example, Panic Disorder, GAD):** the anxiety started before the substance or illness, or lasts long after it stops or is treated.
2. **Adjustment Disorder with anxiety:** worry is a psychological reaction to being ill.
3. **Illness Anxiety Disorder:** fear is about having a disease, without a physiological cause of the anxiety.
4. **Delirium:** disturbed attention and awareness that fluctuate.
5. **Substance intoxication or withdrawal alone:** the anxiety is expected for that state and is not more severe.

## Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders

### Obsessive-Compulsive Symptoms Induced by a Substance or Medical Condition

**Members:** Substance/Medication-Induced Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder; Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

**Core:** Obsessions, compulsions, skin picking, hair pulling or other body-focused repetitive behaviors, caused directly by a substance (for example, stimulants) or a medical condition (for example, PANDAS after a strep infection, basal ganglia injury).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **OCD or a related primary disorder:** symptoms began before the substance or illness, or continue after it ends.
2. **Delirium:** symptoms occur only with disturbed attention and awareness.
3. **Tic Disorders:** sudden movements with a premonitory urge, not done to neutralize an obsession.
4. **Dermatological condition:** picking or scratching is explained by an itchy skin disease.

## Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders

### Reactive Attachment Disorder

**Core:** A young child (developmental age at least 9 months, signs before age 5) rarely seeks or responds to comfort when distressed, with limited positive affect and unexplained irritability or fear, after extremes of insufficient care (neglect, frequent changes of caregivers, institutions).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Autism Spectrum Disorder:** restricted, repetitive behaviors, and selective attachment is usually present; there is no required history of severe neglect.
2. **Intellectual Developmental Disorder:** attachments match the developmental level; comfort-seeking is present.
3. **Depressive Disorder:** the child still seeks and responds to comfort from caregivers.
4. **Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder:** the child is overly friendly with strangers instead of withdrawn.

### Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder

**Core:** A child (developmental age at least 9 months) actively approaches and interacts with unfamiliar adults with little reticence: overly familiar talk or touch, little checking back with the caregiver, willing to go off with a stranger. History of extremes of insufficient care.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **ADHD:** impulsivity also appears in attention and activity across settings, not only as social disinhibition, and without a neglect history.
2. **Reactive Attachment Disorder:** the child is withdrawn and inhibited with caregivers.
3. **Normal friendly temperament:** the child still checks back with the caregiver and is wary of leaving with strangers.
4. **Williams syndrome or other genetic condition:** the social pattern is part of a known genetic syndrome with typical physical findings.

## Dissociative Disorders

### Dissociative Identity Disorder

**Core:** Disruption of identity with two or more distinct personality states (changes in sense of self and agency, behavior, memory, perception), plus recurrent gaps in recall of everyday events, personal information or trauma. Not part of an accepted cultural or religious practice, and not from a substance or medical condition.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Other Specified Dissociative Disorder:** identity disturbance without full distinct states, or without amnesia.
2. **Dissociative Amnesia:** memory gaps without discontinuity of identity.
3. **PTSD:** dissociation is limited to flashbacks and trauma reminders, without distinct identity states.
4. **Psychotic disorder:** voices are experienced as external and come with other psychotic features; reality testing is lost.
5. **Bipolar Disorder:** the shifts are mood episodes lasting days, with a continuous sense of self.
6. **Seizure disorder:** EEG findings and stereotyped episodes with confusion after them.
7. **Factitious Disorder or Malingering:** symptoms are produced on purpose, often dramatized, for an internal or external reward.

## Feeding and Eating Disorders

### Pica

**Core:** Persistent eating of nonnutritive, nonfood substances (for example, paper, soap, chalk, soil) for at least 1 month, inappropriate to the developmental level (usually age 2 or more) and not culturally supported.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Normal developmental mouthing:** a child under about 2 years who mouths objects.
2. **Culturally supported practice:** the eating is part of a sanctioned cultural practice.
3. **Anorexia Nervosa:** nonfood items (for example, tissues) are eaten only to control appetite or weight.
4. **Factitious Disorder:** objects are swallowed on purpose to fake illness.
5. **Nonsuicidal self-injury:** harmful objects (for example, pins) are swallowed to harm the self.

### Rumination Disorder

**Core:** Repeated regurgitation of food for at least 1 month; the food may be re-chewed, re-swallowed or spit out. Effortless, without nausea, and not due to a gastrointestinal or medical condition.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Gastrointestinal condition (for example, reflux, pyloric stenosis):** tests find a structural or medical cause.
2. **Bulimia Nervosa:** regurgitation is done on purpose to control weight, with concern about shape.
3. **Anorexia Nervosa:** regurgitation is part of restriction driven by fear of weight gain.
4. **Vomiting from nausea:** the person feels nauseated and retches; rumination is effortless.

## Elimination Disorders

### Enuresis

**Core:** Repeated voiding of urine into bed or clothes, at least twice a week for 3 months or with clear distress, at age 5 or older (or the same developmental level). Subtypes: nocturnal, diurnal, or both.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Neurogenic bladder or structural problem:** the wetting is explained by a neurological or urological finding.
2. **Medical condition (for example, diabetes, urinary tract infection, seizures):** thirst, frequency, pain or seizures point to the medical cause.
3. **Medication side effect (for example, antipsychotics, diuretics):** the wetting started with the medication.
4. **Developmentally normal wetting:** the child is under 5 years old.

### Encopresis

**Core:** Repeated passage of feces in inappropriate places, at least once a month for 3 months, at age 4 or older (or the same developmental level). Subtypes: with constipation and overflow, or without.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Medical condition (for example, Hirschsprung disease, hypothyroidism, spinal lesion):** the soiling is explained by a medical finding.
2. **Laxative or medication effect:** the soiling happens only with the substance.
3. **Developmentally normal accidents:** the child is under 4 years old.
4. **Oppositional or Conduct Disorder alone:** the soiling is a deliberate act within a broad pattern of defiance; diagnose both only if criteria for each are met.

## Sleep-Wake Disorders

### Narcolepsy

**Core:** Recurrent periods of an irrepressible need to sleep, lapses into sleep or naps, three times a week for 3 months, plus cataplexy, low hypocretin in the spinal fluid, or REM sleep latency of 15 minutes or less (or two or more sleep-onset REM periods on testing).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Hypersomnolence Disorder:** no cataplexy and no early REM periods on testing; naps are long and unrefreshing.
2. **Insufficient sleep:** sleepiness ends with longer nights of sleep.
3. **Breathing-Related Sleep Disorder:** snoring and apneas on a sleep study explain the sleepiness.
4. **Seizures or syncope:** attacks have EEG or cardiac findings, not emotion-triggered muscle weakness with clear consciousness.
5. **Major Depressive Disorder:** sleepiness occurs with depressed mood and no cataplexy.

### Breathing-Related Sleep Disorders

**Members:** Obstructive Sleep Apnea Hypopnea; Central Sleep Apnea; Sleep-Related Hypoventilation

**Core:** Sleep disruption from abnormal breathing during sleep, shown by polysomnography: obstructive apneas and hypopneas (with snoring, snorting or gasping, or daytime sleepiness), central apneas without breathing effort, or decreased breathing with high CO2.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Primary snoring:** snoring without apneas, hypopneas or daytime symptoms.
2. **Insomnia Disorder:** trouble falling or staying asleep without breathing events on the study.
3. **Narcolepsy:** cataplexy and early REM periods; little snoring or obesity link.
4. **Panic attacks at night:** the person wakes with fear and racing heart but the sleep study shows no apneas.
5. **Medication or substance (for example, opioids):** central apneas start with the substance.

### Circadian Rhythm Sleep-Wake Disorders

**Members:** Circadian Rhythm Disorder, Delayed Sleep Phase Type; Circadian Rhythm Disorder, Advanced Sleep Phase Type; Circadian Rhythm Disorder, Irregular Sleep-Wake Type; Circadian Rhythm Disorder, Non-24-Hour Type; Circadian Rhythm Disorder, Shift Work Type

**Core:** A persistent or recurrent pattern of sleep disruption from a mismatch between the body clock and the required sleep-wake schedule, causing insomnia or excessive sleepiness and distress or impairment. Sleep is normal when the person keeps their own schedule.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Normal variation in sleep timing ("night owl"):** no distress or impairment.
2. **Insomnia Disorder:** poor sleep continues even when the person sleeps on their preferred schedule.
3. **Voluntary late schedule:** the person chooses the late hours and can change them without trouble.
4. **Depressive or Bipolar Disorder:** sleep timing changes only during mood episodes.
5. **Breathing-Related Sleep Disorder:** apneas on a sleep study explain the sleepiness.

### NREM Sleep Arousal Disorders

**Members:** NREM Sleep Arousal Disorder, Sleepwalking Type; NREM Sleep Arousal Disorder, Sleep Terror Type

**Core:** Recurrent incomplete awakenings, usually in the first third of the night, with sleepwalking or sleep terrors (abrupt panic with screaming). Little or no dream recall, hard to comfort during the episode, and amnesia for it afterward.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Nightmare Disorder:** the person wakes fully, is oriented, and remembers a vivid dream, usually late in the night.
2. **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder:** dream-enacting movements later in the night, the person wakes quickly and recalls the dream; REM without atonia on testing.
3. **Nocturnal seizures:** stereotyped episodes with EEG findings.
4. **Panic attacks at night:** the person wakes fully and remembers the panic.
5. **Substance-induced (for example, sedative-hypnotics):** episodes start with the medication.

### Nightmare Disorder

**Core:** Repeated long, very unpleasant, well-remembered dreams (threats to survival or security), usually in the second half of the night. On waking the person is quickly alert and oriented. The nightmares cause distress or impairment.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **NREM Sleep Arousal Disorder (sleep terrors):** early in the night, little dream recall, hard to wake.
2. **PTSD:** nightmares replay the trauma and come with other PTSD symptoms (then nightmares are part of PTSD unless they need separate attention).
3. **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder:** complex movements act out the dreams.
4. **Substance or medication (for example, withdrawal from REM-suppressing drugs):** nightmares start with the substance change.
5. **Occasional bad dreams:** no distress or impairment.

### REM Sleep Behavior Disorder

**Core:** Repeated arousal during sleep with vocalization or complex movements that act out dreams, in the later part of the night. On waking the person is alert and recalls the dream. Polysomnography shows REM sleep without atonia. Often an early sign of a synucleinopathy (for example, Parkinson's, Lewy body disease).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **NREM Sleep Arousal Disorder:** episodes in the first third of the night, confused on waking, no dream recall.
2. **Obstructive sleep apnea:** movements happen with apneic arousals and REM atonia is normal.
3. **Nocturnal seizures:** stereotyped episodes with EEG findings.
4. **Medication-induced (for example, antidepressants):** symptoms start with the medication.

### Restless Legs Syndrome

**Core:** An urge to move the legs with uncomfortable sensations, which starts or worsens at rest, is relieved by movement, and is worse in the evening or at night. Three times a week for 3 months, with distress or impairment.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Leg cramps:** a painful, palpable muscle contraction relieved by stretching.
2. **Positional discomfort:** relieved by changing position, without an urge to move.
3. **Akathisia:** restlessness of the whole body after starting a dopamine-blocking drug, not worse at night.
4. **Peripheral neuropathy:** pain or numbness not relieved by movement and not worse in the evening.
5. **Habitual foot tapping:** no urge or uncomfortable sensation.

## Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders

### Pyromania

**Core:** Deliberate fire setting on more than one occasion, with tension before and pleasure or relief after, and fascination with fire and its context. Not for money, revenge, ideology, or to hide a crime, and not in response to a delusion or impaired judgment.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Fire setting for gain, revenge or sabotage:** a clear motive, without the tension-and-relief pattern.
2. **Conduct Disorder or Antisocial Personality Disorder:** fire setting is one of many antisocial acts.
3. **Developmental experimentation:** a child playing with matches or lighters out of curiosity.
4. **Manic Episode:** fire setting only during mania.
5. **Psychotic disorder:** fire set in response to a delusion or command hallucination.
6. **Neurocognitive or Intellectual Developmental Disorder:** impaired judgment explains the act.

### Kleptomania

**Core:** Recurrent failure to resist impulses to steal objects that are not needed for personal use or for their value, with rising tension before and pleasure or relief at the time. Not done from anger, revenge, a delusion or a hallucination.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Ordinary theft or shoplifting:** the item is wanted for its use or value, or the act is planned.
2. **Malingering:** the person fakes the symptoms to avoid prosecution.
3. **Conduct Disorder or Antisocial Personality Disorder:** stealing is part of a general antisocial pattern.
4. **Manic Episode:** stealing only during mania.
5. **Psychotic disorder:** stealing in response to a delusion or hallucination.
6. **Major Neurocognitive Disorder:** items are taken without understanding or remembering.

## Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders

### Substance Intoxication

**Members:** Alcohol Intoxication; Cannabis Intoxication; Opioid Intoxication; Stimulant Intoxication; Sedative, Hypnotic, or Anxiolytic Intoxication; Caffeine Intoxication; Phencyclidine Intoxication; Other Hallucinogen Intoxication; Inhalant Intoxication

**Core:** A reversible syndrome from recent use of a substance, with problematic behavioral or psychological changes and class-typical physical signs (for example, slurred speech and unsteady gait with alcohol, pinpoint pupils with opioids, dilated pupils and tachycardia with stimulants, red eyes and appetite with cannabis).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Another substance class:** the physical signs fit the other class (pupils, heart rate, gait, speech).
2. **Substance Withdrawal:** symptoms began after stopping or cutting down, not after use.
3. **Substance-induced mental disorder (for example, psychotic, depressive):** symptoms are more severe than expected for intoxication and need attention on their own.
4. **Medical condition (for example, hypoglycemia, head injury, diabetic ketoacidosis):** tests or exam find the medical cause.
5. **Delirium:** disturbed attention and awareness are the main problem.

### Substance Withdrawal

**Members:** Alcohol Withdrawal; Cannabis Withdrawal; Opioid Withdrawal; Stimulant Withdrawal; Sedative, Hypnotic, or Anxiolytic Withdrawal; Tobacco Withdrawal; Caffeine Withdrawal

**Core:** A substance-specific syndrome after stopping or reducing heavy, prolonged use, with distress or impairment (for example, tremor, sweating and seizures with alcohol or sedatives; yawning, dilated pupils, gooseflesh and diarrhea with opioids; low mood, fatigue and vivid dreams with stimulants; irritability and craving with tobacco).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Substance Intoxication:** the symptoms follow recent use, not stopping.
2. **Another substance class:** the signs fit the withdrawal of a different substance.
3. **Anxiety or depressive disorder:** symptoms started before the use, or last well beyond the expected withdrawal period.
4. **Medical condition (for example, hyperthyroidism, infection):** tests or exam find the medical cause.
5. **Delirium (for example, withdrawal delirium):** disturbed attention and awareness that fluctuate; then diagnose withdrawal delirium.

## Neurocognitive Disorders

### Neurocognitive Disorder Etiological Subtypes

**Members:** Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Alzheimer's Disease; Frontotemporal Neurocognitive Disorder; Neurocognitive Disorder With Lewy Bodies; Vascular Neurocognitive Disorder; Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Traumatic Brain Injury; Substance/Medication-Induced Neurocognitive Disorder; Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Parkinson's Disease; Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Huntington's Disease; Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Prion Disease; Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection

**Core:** A Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder whose pattern and evidence point to one cause: Alzheimer's (slow, steady decline starting with memory and learning), frontotemporal (early behavior or language change), Lewy bodies (fluctuating attention, visual hallucinations, parkinsonism, REM sleep behavior disorder), vascular (stepwise course linked to strokes), TBI (right after a head injury), and others with their own findings.

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Alzheimer's disease:** insidious onset with early memory loss and steady decline, without stepwise changes or early behavior change.
2. **Frontotemporal degeneration:** early disinhibition, apathy, compulsions or language loss, with memory relatively spared; often younger onset.
3. **Lewy body disease:** fluctuating cognition, well-formed visual hallucinations and parkinsonism within a year of cognitive decline.
4. **Vascular disease:** stepwise decline linked in time to strokes, with imaging evidence.
5. **Parkinson's disease:** parkinsonism clearly started at least a year before the cognitive decline.
6. **Delirium:** acute onset with fluctuating attention.
7. **Major Depressive Disorder:** cognitive complaints improve when the depression is treated.

## Medication-Induced Movement Disorders and Other Adverse Effects of Medication

### Medication-Induced Movement Disorders

**Members:** Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome; Medication-Induced Acute Dystonia; Medication-Induced Acute Akathisia; Tardive Dyskinesia; Medication-Induced Parkinsonism; Antidepressant Discontinuation Syndrome

**Core:** A movement or physical syndrome caused by a medication, recognized by its timing: NMS (fever, severe rigidity, confusion and high CK after a dopamine blocker), acute dystonia (sustained muscle spasm within days of starting), akathisia (restlessness within weeks), parkinsonism (tremor, rigidity, slowness within weeks), tardive dyskinesia (involuntary movements after months of use), and discontinuation syndrome (flu-like and sensory symptoms after stopping an antidepressant).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Serotonin syndrome:** clonus, hyperreflexia and diarrhea after serotonergic drugs, with quick onset.
2. **Infection or heat stroke:** fever with a found infection or heat exposure, without lead-pipe rigidity.
3. **Anxiety or agitation from the illness:** restlessness is psychological, without the motor urge after a dose change.
4. **Parkinson's disease:** symptoms began before any dopamine-blocking medication and keep progressing.
5. **Tic or other primary movement disorder:** movements predate the medication.
6. **Relapse of the original disorder:** after stopping an antidepressant, symptoms start slowly, without flu-like or sensory symptoms, and do not fade within weeks.

## Other Conditions That May Be a Focus of Clinical Attention

### Conditions That Are Not Mental Disorders

**Members:** Malingering; Uncomplicated Bereavement

**Core:** A focus of clinical attention that is not a mental disorder: Malingering (intentional false or exaggerated symptoms for an external reward, such as avoiding duty, getting money or drugs, or escaping prosecution), or Uncomplicated Bereavement (an expected grief reaction to a death, with waves of sadness tied to reminders, without a full disorder).

**Differentials (what rules each one out):**

1. **Factitious Disorder:** deception without an obvious external reward.
2. **Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder:** symptoms are not produced on purpose.
3. **Major Depressive Disorder:** pervasive low mood, worthlessness and impairment beyond grief, for 2 weeks or more.
4. **Prolonged Grief Disorder:** intense yearning or preoccupation 12 months or more after the death, with impairment.
5. **Adjustment Disorder:** distress out of proportion to the loss, or impairment, without another disorder.
